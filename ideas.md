# Portfolio Design Direction

## Three Stylistic Approaches

### Theme Name: Editorial Research Notebook
Very brief intro: A warm, paper-like portfolio that treats the candidate's work as a sequence of evidence-led notes, case studies, and academic milestones. It feels calm, literate, and human while remaining technical.
Probability: 0.07

### Theme Name: Quiet Systems Studio
Very brief intro: A restrained light interface built around diagrams, modular system blocks, and precise annotation. The tone is polished and computational without drifting into futuristic or corporate SaaS styling.
Probability: 0.04

### Theme Name: Terracotta Signal
Very brief intro: A modern light portfolio with warm ivory surfaces, terracotta markers, and a confident editorial rhythm. It balances graduate-school credibility with practical engineering energy.
Probability: 0.08

## Chosen Approach: Quiet Systems Studio

### Design Movement
Contemporary editorial minimalism with influences from Swiss information design and research notebook interfaces.

### Core Principles
1. Present evidence before adjectives: projects, publication, education, and experience carry the narrative.
2. Use asymmetry and annotation: offset columns, numbered sections, side notes, and connector lines create a systems-minded reading path.
3. Keep the interface warm and legible: ivory backgrounds, charcoal text, restrained terracotta, and sage accents replace dark AI clichés.
4. Use motion as signal: short, quiet reveals and tactile hover states support scanning without distracting from content.

### Color Philosophy
Warm ivory (#F5F1EA) is the primary field because it feels archival and considered. Deep charcoal (#282723) provides academic seriousness. Terracotta (#B96545) is the ownable signal color for active links, publication markers, and key accents. A muted sage (#7C8B75) marks learning, systems, and secondary metadata. No gradients are needed; depth comes from layering, linework, and soft shadows.

### Layout Paradigm
A vertical, annotated reading path rather than a centered hero-plus-grid template. The hero uses an asymmetric split: a left editorial introduction and a right abstract data-to-model visual. Sections alternate between full-bleed paper fields and offset content rails. Projects use case-study bands with workflow diagrams and evidence columns instead of repeated generic cards.

### Signature Elements
- Terracotta index numbers and small uppercase section labels.
- Fine connector rules and node diagrams echoing feature pipelines and research notes.
- A recurring circular mark made from three connected dots, used in the wordmark, favicon, and timeline anchors.

### Interaction Philosophy
Interactions should feel like handling a well-made research folio: links underline or shift slightly, cards lift by a few pixels, and mobile navigation opens as a calm side sheet. Every interactive element remains explicit, keyboard reachable, and understandable without motion.

### Animation
Use 180–260ms ease-out transitions for buttons, links, and cards. On first load, fade and translate key blocks upward by 12px in a staggered sequence. Let the abstract hero diagram gently drift by a few pixels only when reduced motion is not requested. Keep section transitions quiet and never animate layout dimensions.

### Typography System
Use Space Grotesk for display headings and section labels, paired with Source Sans 3 for body copy and metadata. Headings are tight, confident, and mostly sentence case. Body copy stays between 16–19px with generous line height. Labels use 11–12px uppercase with letter spacing. Avoid overly heavy weight: strong hierarchy comes from size, spacing, and terracotta markers.

### Brand Essence
A technically grounded AI & Data Science graduate who turns learning into practical systems, research exposure, and evidence-led projects for academic and professional audiences.
Personality: curious, precise, grounded.

### Brand Voice
Headlines are direct and observant. CTAs are specific and low-pressure. Microcopy sounds like a research-minded practitioner, not a sales page.
Example lines: “Selected work, documented as systems.” “Open to conversations around AI, data, and graduate study.”

### Wordmark & Logo
A compact three-node circular mark, with one terracotta node and two charcoal nodes connected by a thin open curve. The wordmark uses the initials “SSK” in a custom-spaced lockup rather than a default logo treatment.

### Signature Brand Color
Terracotta Signal — #B96545.

## Implementation Notes
- Use only verified content from the provided resume and brief.
- Do not invent missing dates, metrics, URLs, project screenshots, or publication links.
- Missing evidence should be shown as quiet, clearly labeled placeholders where helpful.
- Resume download should point to the provided resume asset after upload.
- GitHub is intentionally omitted until a real URL is provided; LinkedIn and email use verified sources.

## Style Decisions
This direction is intentionally light, warm, restrained, and information-led. Avoid dark themes, neon effects, blue/purple SaaS gradients, fake proficiency bars, and fabricated project metrics.
